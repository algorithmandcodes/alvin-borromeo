Career History

The Journey

34 years. Four Airlines. Three Cruise Lines. Five-Star Hotels. Three Oceans. One consistent standard of excellence.

It started with a set of knives, a starched jacket, and the heat of a Queensland kitchen in 1991. Before the airline networks, before the cruise ship brigades, before the boardrooms and the billion-dollar contracts, there was a first-year apprentice in Cairns learning how to hold a blade correctly and understanding that precision in any operation is never a suggestion.

1991 TO 2001 · LAND

The five-star hotel years shaped the foundation. Accor. Hilton. The kind of operations where a kitchen brigade does not simply run one restaurant. It runs every revenue centre simultaneously. Multiple outlets, room service, banqueting, convention catering, resort dining, all feeding from the same brigade, the same purchasing cycle, and the same cost structure. Here the commercial discipline was learned early: food cost percentages, labour ratios, multiple profit centres, and the understanding that an operation is not just a place of craft. It is a business that must perform financially every single shift.

2001 TO 2009 · SEA

Then came the oceans. Royal Caribbean, Carnival, Holland America. Three cruise lines, four vessels each, three oceans. A cruise ship is unlike any operation that came before. It is a self-contained floating resort carrying nearly 3,000 guests with no resupply until the next port. Cookbooks developed with international culinary teams. Menus engineered for guests from every culture imaginable. Provisioning coordinated from Caribbean, Mediterranean, and Pacific ports under maritime compliance standards that tolerate nothing. The financial model was different again: cost-per-passenger-day, global procurement across rotating port schedules, zero margin for error when the next galley load is 48 hours away. The world revealed itself one port at a time, and so did a deeper understanding of how scale and financial control interact when the operation is moving.

2009 TO 2026 · SKY

Then the skies. Philippine Airlines, Qatar Airways, Saudi Airlines Catering, Oman Air. Inflight catering combines the precision of the hotel, the logistics complexity of the cruise ship, and an entirely different commercial architecture all of its own. Every airline has outstations, cities around the world where meals must be produced and loaded inbound and outbound, to the same standard, whether the operations leader is standing there or not. Meal presentations conducted personally across 131 destinations. Uplift standards set for every route. SLA frameworks negotiated with base catering providers. Production systems built that airlines still run today. The financial controls in aviation are different again: cost-per-meal, catering uplift contracts, commercial negotiations with global suppliers, and the relentless management of compliance and efficiency across a network that never sleeps.

What connects land, sea, and air is not just the food. It is the ability to walk into any size of operation, understand its commercial structure, mobilise its people to a higher standard, and leave it performing measurably better than it was found. Thirty-four years have been spent crossing between these three worlds, carrying the financial and operational discipline from one into the next, and building something that very few in this industry can claim: a complete and proven understanding of hospitality, catering, and commercial leadership at every altitude.

The journey is far from finished. Every chapter has demanded more, and that is exactly how it should be. The ambition to take on bigger challenges, scale further, grow deeper, and reach heights not yet touched remains as strong today as it was in that Cairns kitchen in 1991.
